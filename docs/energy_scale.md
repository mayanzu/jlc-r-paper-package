# Normalized energy scale and transfer assumptions

The following details accompany Section 3.2. Energy parameters, model tolerances and irradiance inputs are unchanged. Citation keys refer to `docs/manuscript_source/references.bib`.

Each node keeps a fixed shading factor $\phi_i$ for persistent spatial differences in solar input (canopy structure, mounting orientation), and the days 5--7 low-supply episode acts as a multi-day cloud-cover stress test. To evaluate resource-coordination dynamics over a tractable 14-day horizon, storage ($B_{max}=4\text{ J}$) and per-round harvest ($H_{peak}=0.12\text{ J}$) use a normalized energy-to-workload scale rather than a specific commercial battery pack. Published agricultural sensing platforms illustrate device diversity: tomato greenhouse nodes use 3.7-V LiPo packs of 5000--10000 mAh with small photovoltaic panels [@sat_tomato2022]; a sugarcane monitoring study reports 74-Wh storage with 10-W panels [@sat_sugarcane2026]; and a LoRa node platform lists 18650 cells with 1.8-W panels [@sat_solar2025]. These references document the range of fielded hardware. The 56-mW idle-listening power is a model parameter chosen for the normalized scale (the 52--53 mA figure reported by Salhi et al.\ [@sat_node2026] corresponds to LoRa transmit burst peaks, not idle listening). The harvesting traces keep the diurnal irradiance dynamics of NOAA SURFRAD.

In an ideal real-arithmetic ledger, a common positive scaling of all energy-valued quantities---storage capacity $B_{max}$, initial and reserve energies ($B_{reserve}=0.15$ J), peak harvest $H_{peak}$, all per-operation power costs, and absolute gates such as the 0.08-J restart threshold and the 0.15-J battery normalization constant in Eq. (18)---preserves the normalized state and decision inequalities, provided the scaling also preserves the storage-to-load ratio, initial-energy-to-deficit ratio, and temporal structure of the workload. The reported evaluation characterizes this specified regime. Numerical implementations use fixed absolute tolerances that are not invariant under arbitrary rescaling. At the S200 mandatory online base cost of 0.97632 J/day, full storage ($B_{max}=4$ J) corresponds to approximately 4.10 days of base expenditure before additional guards and data; the initial-storage-to-net-deficit ratio $3/(0.97632-0.648)\approx 9.14$ days gives a simplified deficit time scale that ignores extra tasks and off periods. These figures characterize the modeled regime, not device lifetime. The supply multiplier $s$ in (3) is likewise a normalized model scalar applied identically to physical harvest and to the causal forecast; the winter sweep in Section 5.5 varies this scalar and is not a calibrated panel-area or irradiance multiplier. The spatial-stress extension rescales the same normalized harvest and is reported only as a stress test.
