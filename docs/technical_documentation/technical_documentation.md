# JLC-R technical and reproducibility documentation

This document describes the RC2 implementation and staged five-method study used in the revised article. The earlier V63 confirmation, agricultural extensions and T/R/S factorial belong to their original implementation and are retained in the prior public version; their numerical results are not relabeled as RC2 evidence. Full analytical statements and proofs are in [analytical_proofs.md](../analytical_proofs.md).

## Implementation identity and inputs

The complete algorithm entry is `code/simulation/jlc_r.py:JLCR`. It fixes `horizon_days=3`, `trigger_mode=reference` and `v63_mechanism=relay_repair`. The frozen parent is `v63_model.py:RelayRepairHYB`; additional code implements bounded recovery, demand-triggered Guard protection and an activity cadence. The original execution-source bytes, including the original V63 entry and path-dependent research wrappers, are mapped in `audit/frozen_source_mapping.json`. Adapted package copies are documented separately and do not replace the original formal records.

The task offers one 4000-bit sample from each of 100 sources per 900-second round, with a four-round expiry and freshness criterion. An unsampled obligation remains in the denominator. Nodes operate on a 300 m by 300 m field; links within 120 m follow the configured distance-dependent decoding model. Capacity is 4 J, initial energy 3 J and communication reserve 0.15 J. The simulator charges sensing, synchronization, listening, control, data, reception, failures and retries. Hardware computation, contention and synchronization acquisition are not modeled.

The inputs are synthetic light and three processed NOAA SURFRAD irradiance trajectories, T1 Table Mountain winter, T2 Table Mountain spring and T3 Bondville winter. They cross supply multipliers 1.0 and 0.7. NOAA provenance and quality records are linked from `data/surfrad/provenance.json`; processing does not make them field measurements of the modeled sensor hardware.

## Controller behavior

At each four-round planning boundary, a past-only scenario bank supports the original integer listening and credit prefix planner. A demand-gated reactive mode can replace an unavailable or thin prefix plan; it does not borrow future energy. Charged request/response transactions, one-hop forwarding holds, local freshness ordering and the original physical affordability checks remain active in both modes.

For each node the activity estimate divides current battery above reserve by three days and adds the next-day causal harvest prediction. Public role schedules estimate head expenditure, member off-state expenditure and the incremental cost of full member activity. The clipped affordable fraction maps to a cadence of one, two or four rounds. Cluster heads and nodes with foreign-source packets or forwarding obligations are exempt from intentional sleep. Off rounds pay the configured off-state cost and create unsampled demand. The four-round fallback schedules an opportunity even if the estimate cannot support it, so this mechanism is not a survival or energy-neutrality theorem.

For member-to-head requests, the original transit energy and transaction margin is preserved. An additional complete Guard check is triggered only when the current paid cache reports an eligible reference upstream head with a queued packet and sufficient announced resources. A failed request retains already incurred costs. At a non-reactive head with a full prefix plan, bounded recovery can try at most two additional paid alternatives when the recorded primary-loop outcome permits. Failed receiver feedback and transaction feasibility are considered; original action, retry, Guard and credit limits remain binding. Neither addition reads future irradiance, future decoding outcomes or sink freshness.

The causal assumptions and precise planning and admission equations are in the manuscript. Execution safety concerns actual debits, capacity and one-hop holds under serialized admission. The nominal three-day activity estimate does not guarantee battery reserve, end-to-end delivery, fairness or uninterrupted monitoring.

## Methods and adaptation

The five groups are complete JLC-R, EADCM-CE, LANTERN-CE, base Hybrid and the earlier V63 JLC-R. The two internal controls represent progressive configurations, not independent attribution of every new module. EADCM retains its multi-day duty policy with a common causal forecast and reference FIFO transport. LANTERN retains its energy metric, learning, hysteresis and dynamic upward parent choice, using rates from the previous completed macro-round and a charged logical RPL/MAC abstraction. Original author systems and their field or Cooja performance are not claimed to be reproduced. The complete source-to-rule audit, parameter search and adaptations are in [external_baseline_audit.md](../external_baseline_audit.md).

## Study and statistical protocol

Five methods cross four inputs, two supply multipliers and twelve paired network seeds over fourteen days. The complete controller and two external methods contribute 288 new runs; the earlier JLC-R and base Hybrid contribute 192 previously recorded, hash-checked runs. The network seeds were already used in the preceding 576-run study. Development configuration choices used separate 2023 inputs and development seeds; see `audit/seed_registry.csv` and the original locked protocol in `configs/formal_protocol_original.json`.

The independent pairing unit is the network seed. Within each seed, the eight conditions receive equal weight. Coverage and Source P5 against four controls form the Holm-8 primary family. Condition-specific coverage and P5 form Holm-64; Jain fairness and mean AoI across the eight conditions and their aggregate form Holm-72. Nominal paired 95% intervals are not simultaneous confidence bands. Exhaustive 4096-sign-flip sensitivity has its own Holm-8 correction and does not replace the primary paired-t decision. Paired-t inference requires independent seed-level contrasts and an adequate small-sample approximation; sign-flip sensitivity assumes symmetry under the null.

The eight primary differences are positive after Holm-8, with a maximum adjusted p value of 0.000344. Compared with EADCM, however, coverage is lower in three winter cells by 5.69–7.51 percentage points under Holm-64. LANTERN's equal-condition Jain index is 0.857 versus 0.794 for JLC-R. Mean sampling completion is 68.00% for JLC-R versus 85.53% for the earlier internal control; fixed-demand packet delivery is 23.83% versus 19.06%. These costs must accompany the relative gains.

The observed longest-gap metric is calculated from the same saved trajectories and includes observation-boundary intervals. In T3/0.7, JLC-R's across-source P95 of the longest observed unfresh interval averages 261.27 hours across seeds; this is an observed-window statistic, not a seasonal or field-lifetime estimate. It does not add independent replicates.

## Verification and reproduction

`python scripts/reproduce.py --mode verify` checks package hashes, 480 seed records, 48000 node records and all 152 stored statistical rows without simulation. Node records carry coverage and mean age derived from saved delivered-birth arrays, plus original energy ledger fields. The original round-level arrays, node ledgers and available method-specific logs are retained in the offline research archive rather than bundled. `--mode reproduce` regenerates the statistical tables, 40 condition means and the paper's five figure assets without simulation. `--mode rerun --study confirmation` writes round-level latest-delivery and battery arrays, terminal sample statuses, node ledgers and applicable planning or control logs to a new directory outside the package; it does not produce a complete packet-event trace. The original seeds and task are reused, so a rerun is not independent new confirmation.

The package's path-adapted source was compared against archived records over 24 short replays, covering three new methods and all eight conditions with one already-used seed. A separate full 14-day technical replay of one RC2 case matched the saved arrays, metrics and relevant CSVs after correcting a portable wrapper's terminal-state export order. See `audit/short_replay_equivalence.json`, `audit/full_replay_equivalence.json` and `audit/technical_replay_history.md`. These checks do not prove equivalence for every untested input or state.

## Limitations

Only three measured irradiance trajectories and one synthetic input are considered; all drive simulated networks rather than deployed nodes. Capacity, charging and radio costs are normalized or literature-derived rather than calibrated to a specific hardware stack. The modeled serialized transaction layer does not validate collisions, hidden terminals, physical synchronization, fragmentation or real protocol overhead. The study does not test agronomic outcomes, frost alerts or crop productivity. Long monitoring gaps and reduced raw sampling are material limitations for tasks requiring continuous or complete records.
